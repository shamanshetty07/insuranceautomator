# Adversarial claim matrix

Tests: `backend/tests/test_adversarial_verification.py` (122 tests, no database, no model, no downloads).
These are **synthetic policy tests**. They show what the software does with given inputs. They say nothing about how accurate Qwen-VL or the detector is.

Layers:

- **P** is the application policy, `evaluate_claim` (claim-policy-6).
- **E** is evidence integrity: alias parsing, `check_citations`, `detection_signal`, `text_mentions_damage_type`, and handling of UNRESOLVED association.
- **X** is escalation: `_ask_verifier` with a fake or Qwen-adapter verifier, then `evaluate_claim`, then `Disposition`.

Test classes: `TestPolicyLayer` (P), `TestEvidenceIntegrityLayer` (E), `TestEscalationLayer` (X).
Parametrised case ids are given in brackets.

## What these tests do not show

- **Model accuracy.** They do not show how often Qwen-VL or the YOLO detector is right or wrong. Every verdict and detection signal is synthetic.
- **Rates.** They do not show how often real reports hit each case, or the accept, review and reject rates on real data. That is the experiment protocol's job.
- **Prompts.** They do not show that the prompts stop a real model from fabricating aliases or answering off-vocabulary. They only show that such output is rejected.
- **The DB path.** They do not cover the DB-backed `associate_claims` path or the persistence of runs and rows. That is in the DB-gated tests, which were skipped here because `TEST_DATABASE_URL` is unset.
- **Corroboration catching a bad verifier.** "Corroborated" means detector class or box agreement plus independent support. When both signals are wrong in the same way, nothing in the system notices.
- **Image quality.** No blur or resolution threshold exists. The tiny and blurred images only show that the system does not upscale them and that it relies on the verifier to say `unverifiable`.
- **Stability.** They do not test repeatability of a real model under greedy decoding. The flip-flop test only shows that the software has no cross-run check.
